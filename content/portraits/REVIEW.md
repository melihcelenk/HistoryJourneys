# Portrait review

Which pictures of the figures are used, which are not, and why. The rule is in
`content/rules/RULESET.md`, section 8; the reasoning behind it is in
`docs/tasks/HJ-1/PORTRAITS_ANALYSIS.md`.

- **Reviewed:** 2026-11-24, by the language model that generated the content; not yet checked by a person.
- **Method:** For each figure the image named by Wikidata (property P18) was looked up on Wikimedia Commons. Its
  licence, author, date, source and licence categories were read from the metadata of the file, and the picture
  itself was looked at. File pages were not read in full.
- **Result:** 42 of 51 figures have a portrait; the other 9 are shown with the emblem of their kind.
- **Added 2026-11-25:** Constantine I. Of the four pictures Wikidata names, three are photographs of statues and
  are not used; the fourth, a detail of a mosaic of about 1000 in Hagia Sophia, was accepted. The picture was looked at.
- **Second look:** The first review asked that the date or the author of every work be documented on its file page,
  and accepted 28. That left out Atatürk and nine sultans whose pictures are plainly old works. The rule was widened
  (R21) and 13 more pictures were accepted; for Mehmed II the picture was replaced by the one the owner pointed at.

A picture is accepted only when all of these hold:

1. Commons marks it as public domain or CC0.
2. It is old in fact (R21): the file documents a date before 1900 or an author who died more than 70 years ago; or it
   is a photograph dated more than 70 years ago by an unknown author; or the figure died more than 70 years ago and the
   picture is plainly a work of an earlier age.
3. The picture shows the figure alone and is not a photograph of a statue, a bust or a building.

## What is not certain

- The licence marks on Commons are statements of the people who uploaded the files. Wikipedia relies on the same
  marks; so does this project.
- The photographs of Atatürk (1932) and İsmet İnönü (1938) are out of copyright in Turkey, where an anonymous work is
  protected for 70 years from publication. Their status in the United States is less clear: works that were still
  protected at home in 1996 may have had their protection restored there. Commons hosts them as public domain.
- The undated portraits of sultans are taken to be old from what they look like. Their dates and makers were not
  traced.
- Whether each picture really shows the person it is said to show was not checked against other sources.
